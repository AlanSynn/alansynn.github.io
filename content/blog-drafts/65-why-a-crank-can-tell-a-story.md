# Why a Crank Can Tell a Story

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** I. Craft, mechanisms, and fabrication | **Candidate tags:** computer-science, participatory-design

Turn the crank of an automaton and several things happen. A figure raises its hand, pauses, and lowers it. Another part moves at a different point in the rotation. The maker has arranged these events so that the motion feels like a small story.

I find this an appealing connection between mechanical design and animation. The crank provides a common progression through time. A mechanism can translate that progression into several movements, each with its own timing and range. The relationship between those movements can be as important as the path of any one part.

Someone designing a mechanism for a specific trajectory may want to minimize the error between its movement and a target curve. A storyteller may care more about the relation between two moments. The hand needs to rise before a door opens; the figure needs to pause long enough for the viewer to notice the expression. The technical and expressive constraints meet in the same object.

A design tool can help by making these relationships visible and adjustable. It can also help a maker understand which timing relationships require a different mechanism, and which can be changed within the current one. More automation in mechanical synthesis could give people much greater access to this form of expression.

Not every expressive sequence can be realized with a single crank. Part of the research is to understand the design space created by a particular form of actuation. I would like to see more work that treats such constraints as a medium through which people can compose movement, rather than only as limits on what a mechanism can achieve.

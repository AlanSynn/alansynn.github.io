# The Cost of a Perfect Default

**Status:** editorial draft / argument development | **Theme:** HCI, institutions, and value | **Candidate tags:** participatory-design, ethics, ai

A default that satisfies almost everyone can be remarkably useful. Its success can also make it difficult to discover what people would have wanted if different possibilities had been presented to them.

## Working argument

A generative tool can produce a credible website, image, or explanation immediately. Many people may accept the first result because it is adequate and easy to use. Over time, their accepted outputs can become data for the next version of the system. Popularity then reflects both what users preferred and what they had a chance to encounter.

This creates a feedback loop in which a system's proposed possibilities help shape the evidence used to justify its future defaults. A choice that was never offered receives little feedback. A minority style may remain unusual because the tools make it difficult to produce. The higher the quality of the default, the less obvious the cost of accepting it may become.

The essay would examine default generation as a form of cultural infrastructure. Useful defaults save time, but a field full of excellent defaults may still have impoverished possibilities for exploration. The tension is between the economic advantage of standardized service and the value of differences that are visible only when alternatives are experienced.

## Challenge

Defaults can democratize capabilities and make experimentation easier. Deliberate constraints can improve creative work. The argument should identify whether a default expands or narrows the available alternatives, rather than treating efficiency or similarity as inherently bad.

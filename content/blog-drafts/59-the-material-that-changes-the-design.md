# The Material That Changes the Design

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** I. Craft, mechanisms, and fabrication | **Candidate tags:** computer-science, participatory-design

The behavior of a material can change the idea a maker started with. Suppose a cardboard part bends unexpectedly after fabrication. The maker might choose a stiffer material to preserve the original movement, or discover that the bend gives the object an expressive quality they would like to develop. The physical result has become a source of design information.

The usual account of fabrication treats the digital design as a plan that the material should realize. In this example, the material becomes part of developing the plan. Its behavior offers information that the maker can use to revise the idea.

That relationship gives fabrication systems a reason to support movement between digital and physical work. A useful workflow might let a maker bring a physical modification into the digital design, compare several versions of a part, or explore a change without rebuilding the entire object. The computer helps maintain relationships that are difficult to manage manually. The material helps reveal possibilities that the digital representation did not anticipate.

We should distinguish a productive deviation from a fabrication failure. If a part needs to carry a particular load, an unexpected bend may be unacceptable. In creative work, the same property can become a new expressive device. The difference depends on the intended use and on what has to remain true about the object.

An unexpected deformation is worth incorporating only if the revised object continues to satisfy the constraints that matter. A load-bearing part and an expressive appendage may have entirely different conditions of acceptability. A useful fabrication workflow would let the maker examine which relations the change affects before deciding whether it belongs to the design.

A fabrication workflow that lets a person bring an interesting physical variation back into the design makes another kind of inquiry possible. The maker can investigate how the material behaves and use that understanding to reconsider the intended object. Fabrication becomes one of the activities through which a design develops, rather than only the operation that realizes an earlier decision.

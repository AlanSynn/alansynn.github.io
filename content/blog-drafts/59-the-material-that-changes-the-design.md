# The Material That Changes the Design

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** I. Craft, mechanisms, and fabrication | **Candidate tags:** computer-science, participatory-design

Someone designs a small figure on a screen and then cuts the pieces from cardboard. One piece bends in a way they had not anticipated. They could replace it with a stiffer material. They might also discover that the bending gives the figure an interesting movement.

The usual account of fabrication treats the digital design as a plan that the material should realize. In this example, the material becomes part of developing the plan. Its behavior offers information that the maker can use to revise the idea.

This is one reason I am interested in fabrication systems that allow people to move back and forth between digital and physical work. A useful workflow might let a maker bring a physical modification into the digital design, compare several versions of a part, or explore a change without rebuilding the entire object. The computer helps maintain relationships that are difficult to manage manually. The material helps reveal possibilities that the digital representation did not anticipate.

We should distinguish a productive deviation from a fabrication failure. If a part needs to carry a particular load, an unexpected bend may be unacceptable. In creative work, the same property can become a new expressive device. The difference depends on the intended use and on what has to remain true about the object.

As automated fabrication becomes more capable, the cost of making alternatives will fall in some settings. I hope researchers will examine what people learn through encountering those alternatives. Making a design real can do more than establish whether the original design worked. It can change what the design is supposed to be.

## Editorial source notes

Reading leads: Donald Schön, The Reflective Practitioner; interactive fabrication; material-driven design. The cardboard figure is hypothetical. Use a documented material-led design practice before generalizing to makers.

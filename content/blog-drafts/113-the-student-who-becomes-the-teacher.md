# The Student Who Becomes the Teacher

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** N. Learning, teaching, and shared practice | **Candidate tags:** education, participatory-design

Explaining a mechanism to another person can reveal something the explainer has not understood. A student who knows why a pair of gears turns in opposite directions may find that they cannot yet explain what happens when a third gear is added. A friend's question creates a new problem to investigate.

Preparing an explanation gives the student a different occasion to learn. Someone has to organize an account in a way another person can use. The other person may bring a different question to the activity, or reveal that a distinction the first person took for granted needs to be explained.

A classroom making activity can offer many occasions for this exchange. One learner understands the digital tool. Another has figured out how to assemble a joint. A third has discovered a useful way to plan the movement. When those learners help one another, their partial understandings become resources for the group.

AI can offer a helpful explanation at any of these moments. It may reduce the time students spend waiting for an expert. An educational design might also preserve opportunities for students to discover that they have something worth explaining to someone else. The social and intellectual consequences of that experience deserve attention.

We should not assume that every learner is ready to teach a particular idea. Incorrect explanations can spread, and some students can be asked to carry an unfair share of the work. Guidance and opportunities to check an explanation remain important.

An educational arrangement could give both learners an opportunity to examine the explanation. The person receiving it might try a related example and ask why the result differs from their expectation. This allows the learner who offered the explanation to reconsider it, and helps prevent a confident but mistaken account from becoming the group's shared knowledge.

In a well-supported shared project, knowledge can move among learners rather than always arriving from the person designated to teach. A child can become someone whose understanding is useful to another child, even while needing help with much of the project. That change in their place within the group is one of the educational possibilities I would like a tool to support.

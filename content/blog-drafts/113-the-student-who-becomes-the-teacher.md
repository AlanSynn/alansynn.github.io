# The Student Who Becomes the Teacher

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** N. Learning, teaching, and shared practice | **Candidate tags:** education, participatory-design

A student explains to a friend why a mechanism will not move. The friend asks a question the student had not considered. In trying to answer it, the student discovers something about the mechanism that had been missing from their own understanding.

Teaching can create an interesting kind of learning. Someone has to organize an account in a way another person can use. The other person may bring a different question to the activity, or reveal that a distinction the first person took for granted needs to be explained.

A classroom making activity can offer many occasions for this exchange. One learner understands the digital tool. Another has figured out how to assemble a joint. A third has discovered a useful way to plan the movement. When those learners help one another, their partial understandings become resources for the group.

AI can offer a helpful explanation at any of these moments. It may reduce the time students spend waiting for an expert. An educational design might also preserve opportunities for students to discover that they have something worth explaining to someone else. The social and intellectual consequences of that experience deserve attention.

We should not assume that every learner is ready to teach a particular idea. Incorrect explanations can spread, and some students can be asked to carry an unfair share of the work. Guidance and opportunities to check an explanation remain important.

The possibility I find interesting is that education can continually change who is in a position to help. A person who is a beginner in one activity may become a source of knowledge in another. A well-designed learning environment can give them room to experience both positions.

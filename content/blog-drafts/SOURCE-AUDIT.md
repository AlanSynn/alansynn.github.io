# Source verification ledger

**Review date:** 2026-11-25. This ledger records what was inspected, not a blanket certification of the library. The 34 distinct URLs in the baseline source inventory were considered in the review below. A number of DOI/website requests failed; alternative copies or bibliographic records were used where available. Sources named only as reading leads in the drafts have not all been retrieved. Each expanded manuscript has a separate claim-level note; these notes govern the exact use of a source. Do not turn the source ledger into an automatic approval for other claims.

## Review levels

- **Passage**: relevant text in a paper, results, methods, or an author-published technical report was available and examined. This is not independent reproduction.
- **Abstract / record**: abstract, institutional publication page, or reliable indexed excerpt was examined. Use only for statements actually supported there; retrieve full methods/results before stronger claims.
- **Project**: author or institution's project report or demonstration. Attribute claims to the authors, distinguish demonstrations from measured generalization, and prefer the paper for exact outcomes.
- **Unresolved**: original link or substantive material could not be inspected. Find the exact source before relying on it. Identity alone is not confirmation of a claim.

For PDF sources the available relevant text and, when retrievable, page images were used. In particular, the MotionSmith page-image request failed although the public paper text was available. No figure or result is claimed to have been independently reproduced. The baseline audit is a snapshot of the draft contents before revision, not a count of verified claims.

## Outstanding source work

A URL was absent from 100 of the baseline drafts, although many of those files named books or research topics in their editorial notes. Those sources are leads, not verified evidence. Review the actual work, confirm title, author, date, edition, DOI, and exact claim location. A study that can only be accessed through its abstract is usable for a narrowly supported statement or as a lead. Replace unsupported exact numbers, causal claims, or historical examples; do not invent quotations or references to make an essay sound academic.

The per-file plan in EDITORIAL-ROADMAP.md specifies the case and main challenge for every manuscript. New primary sources must be added to the manuscript's claim notes as they are inspected. An honest absence of empirical evidence is appropriate for a conceptual argument, provided that examples are clearly hypothetical and conclusions are identified as reasoning rather than established findings.

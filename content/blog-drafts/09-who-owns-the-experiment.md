# Who Owns the Experiment?

**Status:** editorial draft / argument development | **Theme:** The institutions of knowledge | **Candidate tags:** sociology-of-science, academia, ai

If ideas and programs become inexpensive, a scientist can still be excluded from an experiment. The equipment belongs to someone, access is scheduled by someone, and the allowable interventions are decided somewhere. Automated research may change the distribution of scientific ability while making those arrangements more important.

## Working argument

Coscientist provides a bounded example of language-model-directed chemical experimentation: its authors combine planning with access to external tools and experimental automation. The importance of the example is the connection between a generated proposal and an instrument capable of acting on it. A person without the instrument does not acquire the same experimental capability simply by acquiring the language model.

In a future of stronger agents, institutions with experimental infrastructure may explore a larger set of possibilities at lower marginal cost. Their decisions about what to instrument, which conditions to measure, which trials are permissible, and who can use the system will influence the frontier of discovery. The owner of the experiment may exercise a form of intellectual agenda-setting even if little human labor goes into choosing each candidate.

There is a competing possibility. Shared laboratories, remotely accessible equipment, cheap fabrication, and openly maintained simulators could expand the range of people able to investigate real questions. The key variable is the allocation of actionable access, rather than model capability alone. A prediction about democratized research needs evidence about who can actually perform and challenge an experiment.

## Challenge

Infrastructure has always affected science, and autonomous laboratories may lower barriers by reducing operator labor. Identify what changes in degree or kind: access to trials, ownership of experiment histories, and the power to refuse outside investigations. Do not simply repeat that compute matters.

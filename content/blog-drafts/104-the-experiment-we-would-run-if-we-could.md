# The Experiment We Would Run If We Could

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** M. Research as a practice | **Candidate tags:** philosophy-of-science, academia

When designing an experiment, I like to ask what evidence would give me the strongest reason to believe the claim, even if obtaining that evidence is impractical. The answer gives me a way to judge the study I can actually conduct. I know which part of the larger question it addresses and which observations will remain missing.

Suppose we want to know whether a creative tool changes the way someone develops an idea over several months. A short laboratory session can reveal interesting things about the first encounter. It cannot reveal all the ways a person's practice would develop. We might combine that session with observations of continued use and ask a more modest question about the period we can actually study.

The distinction helps keep the claim in relation to the evidence. Rather than treating the short study as an imperfect version of a universal answer, we can specify the part of the larger question it addresses. The work may reveal a useful mechanism that a later study can investigate over a longer period.

A shorter session could be especially useful for examining how a person discovers the available operations. Observations several months later would address whether those operations became part of their practice. These are different claims. Knowing which one the study addresses can help us choose the appropriate observations instead of treating a longer study as the only possible improvement.

AI and automation may make some previously infeasible experiments affordable. Other experiments will remain constrained by the time needed for people, institutions, and physical processes to change. We should not assume a larger number of short trials will always substitute for a longer observation.

A feasible study can be much more informative when we explain why its particular observations are useful. Rather than presenting an expensive long-term question as though a brief session has answered it, we can establish what the session revealed and identify the next evidence that would distinguish the accounts still open.

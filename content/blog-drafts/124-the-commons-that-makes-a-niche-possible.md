# The Commons That Makes a Niche Possible

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** O. Careers, institutions, and the economics of expertise | **Candidate tags:** sociology-of-science, computer-science, ethics

A niche can survive because much of the infrastructure it needs is shared with other niches. A small community might maintain a very particular design tool while relying on a common file format, libraries, and fabrication equipment. It can pursue its own activity without reproducing the entire technological world on which the activity depends.

Inexpensive computation could make more of these arrangements viable. A person can use a general tool to build something for a specific practice. If the practice shares some of the costs of maintaining its infrastructure with other communities, its small size need not make the project impossible.

The interesting question is which parts of the arrangement need to remain common. A standard file format may let people exchange objects among specialized programs. A shared fabrication service may make it possible for a community to produce unusual parts. Each community can develop different values and still benefit from some of the same infrastructure.

Those resources have costs. Someone must maintain them, make decisions about changes, and respond when users have conflicting needs. A provider that initially supports many small communities may later find it profitable to favor the ones that generate the most revenue.

The interests of communities can diverge when a shared tool needs to change. A revision that makes one form of work easier might make another community's artifacts difficult to use. Preserving compatibility, supporting a transition, or allowing a community to maintain an alternative are different arrangements for distributing that cost. The shared infrastructure becomes part of the conditions under which their distinct practices can survive.

Plurality depends in part on the things people can agree to maintain together. A useful shared resource gives small communities room to develop different purposes without making each of them carry the full cost of their infrastructure. Its governance matters because a change to that resource can determine which practices are still feasible.

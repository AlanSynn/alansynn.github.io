# Making the Jig Before Making the Thing

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** I. Craft, mechanisms, and fabrication | **Candidate tags:** computer-science, robotics

A craftsperson who needs to drill several holes might spend some time making a guide before drilling the first one. The guide helps position the tool in relation to the material. It will never appear in the finished object, but it changes how the object can be made.

A jig represents a kind of knowledge. It embodies a decision about where parts should be placed and how an operation should be constrained. Once the jig exists, someone can perform a difficult operation without reasoning about every detail again. A practical result has been moved into the tools available for producing another result.

This changes the unit of evaluation for automation in fabrication. We often evaluate a machine by whether it makes the desired object. The machine may be more useful if it can also produce the tools, fixtures, and intermediate arrangements that make a class of objects easier to create. The problem of making a thing can become the problem of designing the process through which many things are made.

Sometimes a jig takes more effort to build than the original task. Its usefulness depends on how often the operation will recur, how much variation needs to be supported, and whether other people can use it. A very specialized jig can be an excellent tool for one practice and a poor general solution.

The cost of building the guide can be worthwhile when it is shared across projects or people. It may be a poor choice for a single object that will not be made again. A design system that proposes such a guide should have a reason to believe the relevant operation will recur, rather than assume that every repeated measurement deserves another artifact.

A system that can design a jig as well as the final part gives its user something reusable. The next time someone needs to perform a related operation, they may be able to work from the knowledge embodied in the guide. I would like evaluations of fabrication systems to account for the processes they make available to people, alongside the objects they produce.

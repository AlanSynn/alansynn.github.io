# When the Reviewer Has an Agent

**Status:** editorial draft / argument development | **Theme:** The institutions of knowledge | **Candidate tags:** academia, sociology-of-science, ai

Imagine a reviewer receiving a manuscript together with an agent capable of reading the cited work, running an artifact, and finding inconsistencies. The most useful change may not be that the agent writes a longer review. It may change what a reviewer is able to inspect.

## Working argument

The ability to produce polished prose and the ability to check the evidence behind it have different costs. When authors can generate plausible explanations and experiments quickly, reviewing only the explanation becomes increasingly hazardous. Agents could help reviewers inspect assumptions, retrieve comparison methods, trace data provenance, and try counterexamples. A recent large-scale randomized study of automated review feedback reported more informative revised reviews in its evaluated setting, showing one practical route by which AI can already enter review.

The next step is more consequential than improving review text. A paper's artifact could become a locus of contest. An author should be able to explain why an automatically generated criticism applies or does not apply. A reviewer should be able to distinguish an executable finding from a plausible-sounding concern. This creates a new form of evidence exchange that may favor papers whose claims are scoped and whose materials can be inspected.

However, when writing an objection becomes cheap, authors may face an unlimited stream of demands. The authority to decide which questions deserve another experiment remains institutional. A better review process would have to limit demands to the central claims, expose the provenance of machine-produced findings, and preserve the ability to contest an automated assessment.

## Challenge

Automated reviewers can share blind spots, hallucinate sources, and violate confidentiality if manuscripts are improperly transmitted. They might also help under-resourced authors. Distinguish private feedback, formal review, and experimental verification.

# The Exercise That Should Stay Difficult

**Status:** editorial draft / argument development | **Theme:** Education and the next generation of expertise | **Candidate tags:** education, philosophy-of-science

A student spends an hour solving a problem that an agent could solve in seconds. From the standpoint of producing an answer, the hour looks wasteful. From the standpoint of producing a person who can understand a class of problems, it may have a different value.

## Working argument

Learning and immediate performance have different timescales. Manu Kapur's work on productive failure provides an example: in particular mathematics studies, trying to solve a problem before instruction supported conceptual understanding and transfer. The initial struggle alone was not the educational intervention; the later instruction and opportunities to organize the attempted solutions mattered.

As AI becomes capable of removing most difficulty from producing an answer, education will need to distinguish the work that belongs to the learner from the work that can safely be delegated. Some obstacles prevent engagement and should disappear. Others provide occasions for forming and revising a mental model. A student's ability to use an agent to complete a task tells us something different from their ability to recognize when the agent's answer is wrong.

The essay would explore a future in which educational settings deliberately choose which difficulties to preserve. A programming student might use tools freely when implementing a larger project, then work without a particular tool when learning a concept whose exercise requires independent reasoning. The point is to design environments for the capability the learner is supposed to develop.

## Challenge

Difficulty can be unproductive, exclusionary, or simply frustrating. AI tutoring can support productive exploration rather than supply answers. The essay should oppose arbitrary suffering and distinguish the pedagogical purpose of a constraint from its resemblance to older examinations.

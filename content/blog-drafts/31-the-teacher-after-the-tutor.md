# The Teacher After the Tutor

**Status:** editorial draft / argument development | **Theme:** Education and the next generation of expertise | **Candidate tags:** education, sociology-of-science, ethics

Suppose an AI tutor becomes better than a classroom teacher at explaining a difficult concept to each student. A school would have good reason to use it. It would still face a decision about who is to be responsible for the education of the children in the classroom.

## Working argument

A teacher participates in a continuing relationship with students, their peers, and the institution. A classroom must deal with conflict, changes in a child's circumstances, questions of fairness, and decisions about which activities are worthwhile. Biesta distinguishes qualification, socialization, and subjectification when discussing educational purposes. This gives a vocabulary for asking what schools are expected to organize beyond particular learning outcomes.

Evidence that AI tutoring improves learning should be taken seriously. In a 2025 randomized study of undergraduate physics lessons, a carefully designed AI tutor produced better immediate learning outcomes in less time than the comparison active-learning classes. That is a result about a bounded educational intervention. It does not determine who should occupy the role of a child's teacher across a school year.

The forecast is that the functions of teacher and tutor will become more sharply separated. Machines may perform much instructional work. Schools and families may continue to place sustained educational judgment and responsibility with human adults, while assigning them very different day-to-day tasks. If this happens, the role's value will have shifted toward the social institution the teacher inhabits.

## Challenge

Human relationships are expensive, and teacher shortages can create pressure for smaller staffs or fully automated environments. The forecast fails if a human teacher becomes a nominal supervisor who rarely knows the children or if meaningful human involvement becomes available mainly to wealthy families. Do not infer institutional survival from the intrinsic importance of education.

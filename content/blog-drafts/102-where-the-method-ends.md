# Where the Method Ends

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** M. Research as a practice | **Candidate tags:** computer-science, academia

A research paper describes a system that turns a drawing into a working object. To understand what it contributes, I would like to see the drawing with which the process begins and the object with which it ends. I would also like to understand what happened between them.

Perhaps someone prepared the drawing by separating its movable parts. Perhaps a designer adjusted the generated mechanism. Perhaps a technician assembled the pieces using experience that the software could not provide. These activities can belong to a perfectly useful workflow. The contribution becomes clearer when we know which parts the method supports and which parts remain outside it.

As agents gain the ability to connect several tools, boundaries around methods may become harder to recognize. A system can obtain impressive results by calling a model, performing a search, asking for a correction, and passing its output to another program. We should understand what each part makes possible before attributing the entire result to the proposed component.

There is no requirement that an interesting system solve the entire problem. A carefully designed interface between two stages can be a significant contribution. Its value may depend on a skilled person continuing the work afterward.

I think a paper should describe these boundaries as part of the result. What does a person need to provide before using the method? What do they receive afterward? Which operations are left to them? Those questions help readers understand the conditions under which the work might become useful in their own projects.

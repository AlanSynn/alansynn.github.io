# The Prototype That Has to Stay

**Status:** editorial draft / argument development | **Theme:** Graphics, fabrication, and making | **Candidate tags:** participatory-design, sociology-of-science, ethics

A prototype can demonstrate an idea in a research lab. It can also become something another person depends on. At that moment, the prototype acquires a new kind of obligation.

## Working argument

Imagine a small community organization using a fabricated device created during a research project. The device works and the organization finds a place for it in its activities. A year later, a component breaks, the researchers are elsewhere, and the people who use the device cannot open its design or obtain a replacement part. A successful demonstration has created a maintenance problem.

As AI makes prototyping less expensive, I expect the cost of producing an initial artifact and the cost of sustaining it to diverge. A community can receive many useful-looking objects without acquiring the capacity to repair them, adapt them, or decide when they should be retired. The relevant infrastructure includes documentation, material supply, compatible tools, permission to modify, and local knowledge.

This changes how the research value of a fabricated or interactive system may be understood. The experiment's most informative part can begin after the paper is finished, when others try to integrate and change the object without the original builder. An account of that history may reveal capabilities and dependencies that were invisible during the first demonstration.

## Challenge

Not every prototype should become a supported product. Demanding lifetime maintenance of an exploratory artifact could make speculative research impossible. The obligation should depend on what was promised, what reliance was encouraged, and which people bear the costs of ending the project.

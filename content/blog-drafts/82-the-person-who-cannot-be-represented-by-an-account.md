# The Person Who Cannot Be Represented by an Account

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** K. Interaction, delegation, and responsibility | **Candidate tags:** ethics, participatory-design

I can want an assistant to make decisions for me in one activity and want to make every decision myself in another. A personalized system that treats either preference as a stable property of my account may use a reasonable observation at the wrong moment. The scope of the observation is part of what the system needs to understand.

A personalized system often needs to construct a representation of its user. Some properties of that representation may remain stable. Others describe a temporary situation or a relationship with another person. The challenge is to keep those differences from becoming a single fixed account of who someone is.

A preference concerning the length of an explanation could be represented as belonging to a learning task, rather than as a property of the person in every setting. If the person later asks for a detailed account while working, the system can use that request without having to discard everything it learned earlier. Personalization needs an intelligible scope as well as accurate predictions.

Suppose a person often asks an agent to make decisions for them at work. The agent might infer that they prefer delegation. In a creative activity, the same person may want to make every small decision themselves. A useful assistant should know when its earlier inference is relevant, and when to leave the question open.

The scope becomes consequential when a system carries information across several parts of a person's life. Persistent personalization can make interactions easier. It can also cause an assumption formed in one setting to follow the user into another. The person may not know which earlier activities have shaped the response they receive today.

Personalization can become more useful when an inference carries information about the circumstances in which it was made. A person could revise a preference for one activity without establishing a new account of themselves in every other activity. A useful representation of someone can remain deliberately incomplete.

# The Work a Definition Has to Do

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** G. Writing, explanation, and representation | **Candidate tags:** philosophy-of-science, computer-science

When a paper says that a robot is autonomous, I want to know what the statement gives me a reason to expect. Perhaps the robot can finish an assigned task without a person continually controlling it. Perhaps it can also reconsider the task when the environment changes. Both descriptions can be useful; they imply different observations.

A definition helps when it tells us what we should expect to observe. If autonomy means that a robot can finish a task without an operator, we can examine when assistance is required. If it includes the ability to reconsider the task, we need evidence of another kind. A shared word does little to resolve this difference unless the paper also explains the behavior it refers to.

Examples and consequences can help establish these boundaries. A definition of a design space becomes clearer when we can see a design that belongs to it and one that does not. A definition of a usable mechanism becomes clearer when we know which changes would make the mechanism unusable. The boundary gives the term a purpose.

For instance, two researchers may agree that a robot completed a task without intervention. They can still disagree about whether the robot could determine when intervention was necessary. The apparent disagreement about autonomy becomes easier to examine when each person identifies the additional observation their definition requires.

There are cases where established terminology should be preserved. Replacing precise technical terms with everyday synonyms can make an argument less accurate. The challenge is to introduce the term when the reader has enough of the underlying idea to recognize it.

A useful definition identifies the relationship a writer intends to discuss and the boundary at which a reader should reconsider applying it. Researchers can use different definitions for different purposes. Making the commitments of each definition visible gives them a better opportunity to understand one another's evidence.

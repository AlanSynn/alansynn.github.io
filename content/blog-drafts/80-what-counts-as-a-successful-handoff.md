# What Counts as a Successful Handoff?

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** K. Interaction, delegation, and responsibility | **Candidate tags:** participatory-design, sociology-of-science

A handoff has succeeded when the next person can do something useful with what they received. A file can arrive on time and be perfectly intact while leaving its recipient unable to recognize which decision should be made next. The information needed for a handoff depends on the activity it is supposed to continue.

A good handoff carries some understanding of the state of a project. Which decisions have been made? Which conditions are still uncertain? What should the next person avoid changing without further investigation? The answers depend on what the recipient is expected to do, rather than on the amount of information the previous person can provide.

Agents will make it easier to produce records of work. They may also complete substantial portions of projects independently. A person who receives the result may need to reconstruct an unfamiliar process, decide which parts to trust, and take responsibility for its next use. A report that everything passed the tests may not provide what they need.

A handoff for debugging may need a record of failed attempts and the reason a workaround was introduced. A handoff for routine operation may need a much smaller account of the current state and the conditions under which someone should seek help. The useful level of detail depends on what responsibility has been transferred.

We can study collaboration through the recipient's next activity. Rather than evaluate a handoff by the speed of transferring information, examine whether the recipient can perform the next meaningful activity. Can they revise a decision, investigate a problem, and recognize a condition that requires help?

For a project that has to survive changes in people or tools, I would test the handoff through a concrete next task. Can the recipient make an appropriate revision, explain why a constraint matters, or recognize when another person needs to be involved? The transfer of an artifact is part of a successful handoff; the transfer of a useful opportunity to act is what makes it consequential.

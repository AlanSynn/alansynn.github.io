# A Model Student Is Not an Average Student

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** N. Learning, teaching, and shared practice | **Candidate tags:** education, ai, ethics

An adaptive learning system can make an early judgment about a student and then decide which evidence of that student's ability it will get to see. If it concludes that a learner needs easier exercises, the learner may have few opportunities to demonstrate an understanding of a more difficult problem. Personalization can therefore shape the data used to justify later personalization.

Perhaps the student understands the idea but has misunderstood the question. Perhaps the exercise assumes knowledge they have not encountered. Perhaps the example is expressed through a representation that they find difficult to use. The general pattern provides a useful starting point for an investigation rather than a complete account of this learner.

A model of a student can help organize what a system knows about their progress. Its usefulness depends on what the model represents and how it can change. A low estimate of mastery could lead a system to provide more practice when the student needs a different explanation. The student's subsequent behavior may then reflect the intervention rather than only their original understanding.

This creates a feedback problem. When a model decides which learning activities someone encounters, those activities help determine the evidence through which the model will understand that person later. A student may have few opportunities to demonstrate a capability the system does not expect them to possess.

We can distinguish an intervention designed to help the learner from an opportunity to examine the model's prediction. Occasionally presenting a task outside the expected level of mastery might reveal that the earlier account was incomplete. The usefulness of doing so depends on the cost to the learner and the purpose of the lesson; surprise should not become another compulsory test.

An appropriate learner model should help a tutor decide what is worth investigating next. It should remain possible for an unexpected answer or a student's explanation to change the account. The important test of such a model includes the opportunities it gives a person to demonstrate something the model did not already believe about them.

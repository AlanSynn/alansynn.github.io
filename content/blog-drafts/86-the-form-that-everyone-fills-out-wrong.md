# The Form That Everyone Fills Out Wrong

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** K. Interaction, delegation, and responsibility | **Candidate tags:** computer-science, participatory-design, sociology-of-science

Imagine a workplace in which people regularly enter information in the wrong field of a form. A developer might respond by making the form clearer or preventing the incorrect entry. Before doing either, I would like to understand why the practice has developed.

Perhaps the official categories do not describe the work very well. People might use one field to leave information that a colleague needs, because the form offers no other place for it. What looks like an error in the record can be part of a useful local arrangement.

This does not mean we should preserve every workaround. Informal conventions can be fragile, difficult for newcomers to learn, and dangerous when important information is interpreted incorrectly. But removing a workaround without understanding its purpose can make the actual work harder.

As agents begin to handle forms and organizational processes, I expect this issue to become more visible. An agent can follow the official rules consistently. It may still encounter situations in which the people doing the work depend on conventions that the rules do not describe. A better system might help them make those conventions explicit, or give them a reason to develop a different practice.

The interesting research begins with the discrepancy between what an organization says should happen and what people actually do. The task is to understand that discrepancy well enough to know which part of the arrangement should change. Correcting a form is sometimes the easy part.

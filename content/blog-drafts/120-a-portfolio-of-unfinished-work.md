# A Portfolio of Unfinished Work

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** O. Careers, institutions, and the economics of expertise | **Candidate tags:** career-advice, academia

A portfolio usually shows the things someone has finished. I would sometimes like to see the things they stopped working on, and understand why they stopped.

Suppose a designer presents a polished prototype. It is difficult to tell from the prototype alone which alternatives were considered, which constraint was important, or which decisions the designer made after seeing someone use it. The history of the project may reveal an understanding that the finished object does not require its viewer to notice.

An unfinished project can be particularly useful in that respect. Perhaps the person discovered that the original problem was poorly framed. Perhaps they found a much better approach but lacked the time to investigate it. Perhaps they simply made a series of mistakes. These are different accounts, and the distinction matters when we are trying to understand what they learned.

As tools make polished work easier to produce, I expect the process behind an artifact to become more interesting to people evaluating a potential collaborator. I would be cautious, though, about treating an elaborate process record as proof of individual expertise. Such records can also be generated, and a long project can contain very little useful inquiry.

The most informative record might be a small set of moments in which the person's understanding changed. A decision to abandon a design can tell us something when it is connected to the evidence that motivated it. An account of unfinished work can make those decisions visible without requiring every project to end with a success story.

# What Should a Lab Know Together?

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** M. Research as a practice | **Candidate tags:** academia, sociology-of-science

A research group can build a system that no individual member fully understands. That is one of the benefits of specialization. The group still needs a way to recognize relationships among the parts when a decision in one area changes what another person can do.

Suppose a lab develops an interactive robotic system. One researcher knows the controller. Another knows the authoring interface. A third understands how participants use the device. A change that improves one part of the system can have consequences that only become apparent when these accounts are brought together.

This suggests that the shared knowledge of a lab deserves deliberate attention. Meetings and documentation can help people learn what their work implies for others. More importantly, the group needs opportunities to encounter problems together. Watching a person use the system may give all three researchers a concrete event about which their different explanations become useful.

The group might maintain an account of the system's dependencies that each person can use from their own position. The designer needs to know which movement constraints are consequential; the controller engineer needs to know which changes the designer considers acceptable. Sharing those relations can be much more useful than expecting everyone to learn the entire implementation.

Agents may make each specialist much more productive and provide access to the expertise of other fields. That could help a lab develop a shared account. It could also make the specialists less dependent on conversations in which they would have discovered differences in their assumptions.

The kind of knowledge I would like a lab to share is an understanding of where its members need one another. A designer need not understand every line of the controller. They should have a way to recognize when a design decision raises a question the controller engineer can help investigate. Shared knowledge makes specialized knowledge usable across the group.

# What a Teacher Notices Before a Test

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** N. Learning, teaching, and shared practice | **Candidate tags:** education, philosophy-of-science

A test tells a teacher how a student performed under the conditions of the test. An ordinary lesson can reveal another kind of evidence: the way a student approaches a difficulty. Someone who repeatedly refers to an earlier example might be making a useful connection, or might be unable to proceed without copying it. The teacher has a reason to investigate.

Teachers encounter students through many activities. They can observe changes in participation, the kinds of questions someone asks, and how the student approaches unfamiliar material. Some of those observations can reveal a difficulty before it appears in the result of a formal assessment.

A quiet student may understand the material well, and a confident student may have an incomplete understanding. A teacher who notices repeated copying of an example might ask the student to explain why the same operation applies in a new problem. The observation provides a reason to investigate; the student's response supplies further evidence. Familiarity alone cannot settle the interpretation.

These observations suggest a different way to organize assessment. AI systems could gather evidence from many learning activities and help identify useful moments for inquiry. The evidence might be much richer than a series of examination scores. We will need to understand how it is interpreted, who has access to it, and which decisions it is used to support.

I expect the social role of teachers to matter here. A person who knows a student over time can bring the observations into conversation and reconsider an earlier judgment. That capability is connected to the relationship, not simply the amount of data collected.

A teacher's observation becomes useful evidence when it gives the learner and teacher a way to understand a particular difficulty together. A formal test may help with that inquiry; so may another example or a conversation. The result should inform the next educational decision without becoming a permanent explanation of the person who made the mistake.

# What a Teacher Notices Before a Test

**Status:** English working draft | **Theme:** N. Learning, teaching, and shared practice | **Candidate tags:** education, philosophy-of-science

A teacher watches a student work through a problem and notices that the student repeatedly looks at a nearby example. The student may eventually produce the correct answer. The way they reached it gives the teacher another reason to ask what the student understands.

Teachers encounter students through many activities. They can observe changes in participation, the kinds of questions someone asks, and how the student approaches unfamiliar material. Some of those observations can reveal a difficulty before it appears in the result of a formal assessment.

The observations can also be misleading. A quiet student may understand the material very well. A confident student may have an incomplete understanding. A teacher needs opportunities to investigate an interpretation rather than treating familiarity with the student as proof of knowing them.

This is a useful way to think about the future of assessment. AI systems could gather evidence from many learning activities and help identify useful moments for inquiry. The evidence might be much richer than a series of examination scores. We will need to understand how it is interpreted, who has access to it, and which decisions it is used to support.

I expect the social role of teachers to matter here. A person who knows a student over time can bring the observations into conversation and reconsider an earlier judgment. That capability is connected to the relationship, not simply the amount of data collected.

A test provides evidence under particular conditions. An ongoing educational relationship can create other occasions for understanding a learner. The challenge is to use these sources of evidence carefully without allowing either one to define the entire person.
